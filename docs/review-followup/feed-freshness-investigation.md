# V01 — feed freshness verification investigation

The two earlier failures at `89884ab` remain preserved as failed executions. They did not record publication times or termination reasons, so their exact cause cannot be recovered retrospectively. Later passes and the green CI do not explain those runs.

The investigation instruments the actual AuthService publication snapshots, authorization generation, LeaseWatch initialization/rechecks/termination, and real TCP collector boundaries. Logs include wall-clock milliseconds, both feed timestamps and versions, deadline, token expiry and the lease-check result. Instrumentation is opt-in, test-only, and retained as an evidence patch rather than shipped in production.

The same diagnostic test body and release/default-allocator configuration were built from original baseline `a7e2070f3b4346b3e54d552069ff91c56e900130` and reviewed source `89884ab114b22929aa93707361e9e8d52c762bc2`. Three quiet sequential paired rounds passed on each. Three other quiet current runs and six current runs during a Rust build also passed; neither observation proves a historical load cause.

A controlled process-scheduling fault then stops only the newly spawned test process for 4.2 seconds after its real `upToDate` barrier. All three baseline attempts and two of three matched current attempts fail the original assertion. The failing traces show publication of policy version 2/generation 4 while grants version 1 is already four or five seconds old against a three-second window. LeaseWatch reports `GrantsStale`, correctly ends the connection, and the final collector sees EOF. Grants refresh milliseconds later, after termination. Three earlier exploratory current pause attempts show the same failure. The one passing matched current pause is retained: both publications completed before its recheck. The scheduling order can therefore decide the old test's result once its freshness precondition has already been missed.

This is executed evidence of a defective fixture/oracle, not a claim that process suspension or CPU load caused the historical failures. No diagnostic run established rejection of genuinely fresh feeds. V01 remains a verification disposition for reviewer acceptance, with that historical observability limit explicit; no new production security defect or historical causal certainty is asserted.

The replacement separates two obligations. Owned ManualClock tests call the same time-parameterized LeaseWatch predicate used by production. They explicitly establish refresh before each preceding deadline, observe both generation changes and the new deadline, and prove the same watcher survives at the old deadline and beyond the initial window. Each independently unrefreshed feed is valid at age == window and rejected at age > window, with its exact reason and exactly-once termination even when the generation is unchanged. Production retains its existing real-time clock and predicate semantics.

The original named TCP test remains a real-server smoke. Under the ordinary fixture freshness window, each paired publication advances the generation twice and is followed by one uniquely identified append delivered exactly once on the same subscription, with no EOF. It no longer infers freshness from real sleeps or treats two seconds of silence as sufficient proof. Existing stale-feed, revocation and token-expiry negative controls remain unchanged. Hash-anchored provenance records the precise test adaptation; original scenario coverage and architecture/lint baselines are retained.

Final-source test results and every diagnostic attempt are delivered separately in the report and portable evidence bundle.
